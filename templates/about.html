About the Model

Technical overview of the ensemble machine learning model powering the depression risk screening tool.

Model Architecture

This screening tool uses a Voting Ensemble of four classifiers that work together to produce a consensus prediction. By combining multiple algorithms, the ensemble achieves higher accuracy and robustness than any single model.

  • Logistic Regression — Linear baseline model that provides interpretable probability estimates and handles linear decision boundaries effectively.
  • Random Forest — Ensemble of 100 decision trees that captures non-linear feature interactions and ranks feature importance.
  • XGBoost — Gradient-boosted trees optimized for structured tabular data, with built-in regularization to prevent overfitting.
  • Gradient Boosting — Sequential ensemble that corrects errors of previous trees, providing strong predictive performance.

Model Version

The model was trained on a dataset of 27,900 clinical samples and validated using cross-validation. The ensemble achieves an AUC-ROC of 99.2%, indicating excellent discrimination between depression and non-depression cases.

Feature Engineering

The model uses 16 raw input features and 16 engineered features that capture complex interactions:

Raw Features: Gender Age City Profession Academic Pressure Work Pressure CGPA Study Satisfaction Job Satisfaction Sleep Duration Dietary Habits Degree Suicidal Thoughts Work/Study Hours Financial Stress Family History

Engineered Features: Total Pressure, Pressure-Satisfaction Ratio, Sleep Quality score, High Risk flag, Stress Multiplier, Overall Satisfaction, Work-Life Balance, Financial-Academic Stress Interaction, Suicide Risk Severity, Mental Health Vulnerability, CGPA Performance Gap, Perfect Health flag, Extreme Stress flag, CGPA Achievement, and more.

Limitations

This tool is a screening aid, not a diagnostic instrument. It provides a statistical risk estimate based on population-level patterns and may not capture individual clinical nuance. Always consult a qualified mental health professional for diagnosis and treatment.

The model was trained on a specific demographic population; predictions for individuals outside this population may have reduced accuracy.
